The problem

One line. Any length. Always readable from the doorway.

The hero carries the whole room in Mode 1 — it is the two-second answer to "what do I need to know?" But its content is never the same length twice: "21°" one moment, "Leave by 8:20 — 20 minute drive, 6 in traffic" the next. A fixed type size can't serve both. So the size is length-responsive: short lines go big, long lines step down — but never below the distance floor. And the copy is trimmed by the temperament, not the typesetter.

Voice sets the type

The temperament trims, so the type never has to shrink to fit

Phase 10 routes every line through one voice before it is ever set. That isn't only tone — it's a typographic safeguard. A rambling, apologetic line would fall to an illegible size; the phrased line lands in Tier B and reads from the hall.

Anatomy

One glyph, one line, tuned for distance

Face & weight
Barlow Condensed Medium. Condensed fits more legible characters per line on a wall; Medium reads calm where Bold would shout.--font-display · 500
Tracking
Near-zero, a hair positive. Large condensed type tightens naturally; over-tracking would break the word into letters at distance.letter-spacing 0.006em
Leading
Set below single (0.96–1.0). A two-line hero should read as one block, not two rows.line-height 0.96
Glyph ratio
The weather/category icon sits at ~0.8× cap height, optically centred — a colour cue that resolves before the words do.≈ 0.8 × cap
Colour

Shadow

Over any photograph

Legible on the brightest frame and the darkest

The line never controls its background — it floats over whatever photo is up. Three layers guarantee contrast on all of them: a bottom readability gradient, the weather tint, and the type's own soft shadow. Target 7:1; never below AA-large.

Exchange, don't slide

One line replaces another in 700 ms — a cross-fade, never a scroll

When the hero changes, the outgoing line fades as the incoming one rises, on the shared personality.timing('hero') curve. No horizontal motion, nothing to track across the wall — the room simply says a different thing, calmly, the way a person changes the subject.

The rule

If it can't be read from the doorway, it isn't the hero — it's noise.

The type scale, the three layers of contrast, and the one-voice trim all serve a single test: someone glances up from the kettle, three metres away, and knows the one thing that matters. Then looks away.
